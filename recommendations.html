Literacy policy recommendations

Strong literacy policy sets clear principles, supports the people who put them into practice, and leaves room for informed instructional judgment. Each recommendation below is shown with the policy lever it depends on and, where one exists, the indicator in the legislation analysis that speaks to it.

How to read this page. Law establishes responsibilities and protections. Guidance adds specificity. Implementation provides capacity. A linked indicator shows that legislation names something. It never shows how well — not quality, depth, integration, funding or implementation. A note appears only where the coding says something more specific than that. ✦ AFT marks a connection to the AFT resolution on early reading instruction and support; open the marker, then View passage to jump to the wording it rests on in the annotated copy. The nine principles derived from that resolution organise the state practices matrix.

About the ASI crosswalk. Counts and indicator names come from the state practices matrix for enacted legislation, 2019–2025, across the 50 states and the District of Columbia. A jurisdiction is counted once when any of its laws meets an indicator's threshold. A 2 generally means addressed and a 1 mentioned; binary indicators use their own definitions. The five pillars may appear across several laws rather than in one. These counts describe legislative content, not policy quality, implementation or outcomes, and law already in force before 2019 falls outside the window. The bill-level diamond in the matrix marks a law that names the literacy research base. Some recommendations have no direct indicator in the matrix, and are marked as such.
