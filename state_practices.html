EDUCATORTeacher ParticipationFormal teacher participation. Teachers hold a majority on the body that decides, or are consulted on a named literacy instrument — materials, curriculum, standards or a screener list. Being listed as one member of a body, or one stakeholder among many, does not count. These are observable in bill text; much teacher participation is not. Implementation planning, structured consultation, collaborative professional learning, and influence exercised through contracts, state boards or district practice leave no trace in statute — Kentucky’s teacher-majority site-based councils select instructional materials under governance that predates these bills. An empty cell means no formal provision in the legislation, not that teachers are excluded from literacy-policy decisions.value 2 of 0–2
SYSTEMFamily ParticipationA two-rung measure. 1 — parents receive information about their child’s reading: a notice of screening results, a deficiency, or the interventions the child will get (this absorbs the database’s separate Parent Notification variable). 2, the level shown here — meaningful participation or two-way communication: a read-at-home plan, a family literacy program, parent training workshops, or parents helping build the child’s reading plan. 29 jurisdictions inform; 19 go further; 3 do neither.value 2 of 0–2

* The principles are broader than the measures beneath them. The columns record what a bill names; a principle often asks for more than naming. Principle 2 is the clearest case: it calls for instruction that is integrated — the components of reading taught in relation to one another rather than in sequence — but the database can record only whether a bill names the five pillars, oral language and background knowledge, not whether it requires them to be taught together. A dot should be read as evidence that a jurisdiction has engaged a principle, not that it has met it in full. The nine principles shown here are derived from the AFT early reading resolution, which covers more ground than these columns do; the columns are the ASI database’s own measures, unchanged, arranged to show which principle each one speaks to. Source: ASI Literacy Legislation Database, enacted bills 2019–2025. Coding revised September 2026 against the bill texts.
