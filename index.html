Key Findings

What the Data Show

The analysis reveals a mixed but improving landscape — more states are referencing multilingual learners, but inclusion and integration are not the same thing.

1 37 states now reference multilingual learners in their literacy legislation — more than double the 16 states in 2019. Growth came in two diffusion waves (2021 and 2023) after the initial science-of-reading surge, followed by consolidation in 2024–2025.
2 The number of states whose literacy legislation reaches at least one instructional-core domain has risen from 9 in 2019 to 23 in 2025. Cumulative depth has nearly doubled as well — the average state's literacy legislation now addresses 1.68 of the 5 core domains, up from 0.88 in 2019.
3 Only 15 of 37 states achieve broad integration across four or more EL domains, including the instructional core. Even within this group, depth varies considerably — five states earn their broad classification primarily through accumulating thin provisions across multiple bills rather than through integrated legislation.
4 The most consistent pattern in the dataset is siloing: EL provisions and science-of-reading provisions are developed in separate legislative vehicles, producing bills that are individually defensible but collectively incoherent. Nevada and Washington illustrate this most clearly.
5 Connecticut, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota, and California stand out as the states where EL and science-of-reading policy are most consistently held together in statute — demonstrating that integrated EL literacy legislation is achievable, while leaving room to grow.
Methodology

About This Analysis

This analysis draws on two databases maintained by the Albert Shanker Institute: the ASI Literacy Policy Database (549 bills, 51 jurisdictions, 2012–2025) and the ASI EL Literacy Policy Database (148 bills, 37 states + DC, 2019–2025). The EL database was hand-coded by domain using a nine-category framework.

Classification Framework

States are classified along two dimensions: scope (narrow = 1–3 EL domains; broad = 4+) and location (instructional core vs. periphery). Instructional-core domains include instructional/program design, teacher PD, teacher preparation, assessment & screening, and targeted resources & staffing.

Bill Quality Indicators

EL provisions are assessed against three bill-level indicators: flagship designation (primary state literacy bill), five-pillar coverage (phonemic and phonological awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, comprehension), and oral language coverage. Together these capture how embedded EL provisions are in the science-of-reading framework.

Integration vs. Siloing

A provision is classified as integrated when it appears in a bill covering five pillars and oral language. Siloed provisions appear in bills outside this framework. Where both exist for the same domain within a state, both are shown if they address different aspects.

Study Period

The EL analysis covers 2019–2025, coinciding with the surge in science-of-reading legislation. Pre-2019 predecessor flagship bills (Mississippi 2013, Utah 2018) are excluded from the EL analysis as they fall outside the study window.
