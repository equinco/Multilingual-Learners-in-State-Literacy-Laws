Methodology

About These Analyses

All of the analyses on this site are built from one source: the ASI Legislation Database, maintained by the Albert Shanker Institute. It records literacy legislation enacted in the 50 states and the District of Columbia. No bill that failed, died in committee or remains pending appears in it — every row is law.

What Is in Scope

543 enacted laws fall inside the 2019–2025 window. 518 are in scope for analysis; the rest reach literacy only administratively — a cross-reference, a date change, a renaming — and are excluded from every table and figure. 40 of the 518 are a jurisdiction’s flagship literacy law.

How Laws Are Coded

Each law is coded on a common set of indicators. Graded indicators distinguish addressed (2) from merely mentioned (1) and absent (0); binary indicators carry their own definitions. A jurisdiction counts once on an indicator when any of its laws meets the threshold, so repeat enactments add nothing and bill counts are not reported.

Grounding in the Research

Every law is marked for whether it names the literacy research base at all — the science of reading, evidence-based reading, any of the five pillars, or dyslexia. Separately, laws are marked for naming the five pillars, oral language and background knowledge. These show how far a law is anchored in the reading research, not how well.

The Nine Principles

Thirteen indicators are arranged under nine principles derived from the AFT resolution on early reading instruction and support. The principles are broader than the indicators: the columns show whether legislation addresses an element of a principle, not whether the principle is met, nor the quality of the provision or its implementation.

Multilingual Learners (Table 3)

One analysis codes multilingual-learner provisions across nine domains and classifies each jurisdiction by scope and location: broadest means five or more domains with at least a modest presence in the instructional core. Core domains are instruction and programme design, professional development, teacher preparation, assessment and screening, and targeted resources and staffing.

What These Data Cannot Show

These are counts of legislative content. They do not measure quality, funding adequacy, implementation or student outcomes. Law already in force before 2019 falls outside the window, so a jurisdiction whose framework predates it — Mississippi 2013, Utah 2018 — may read thinner here than it is.
